Some of the most painful reviews come from loyal, high-spending regulars, because they are exactly the clients a salon most wants to keep. This report on V Nails & Spa comes from a customer who estimates she spends around $300 a month there and who, after a string of issues, has decided she is done. Her account, shared here as her own experience, points to trust, quality, and listening, and all three are things a salon can protect.

## A Tip the Client Didn't Choose

The concern that stands out most is about billing trust. The client said that during a problem with the card reader, the technician handled the payment and, in doing so, entered a $20 tip for herself. A tip is always the client's decision, an amount she chooses to give, and it should never be added by staff. If a card reader has issues, the client should still be the one to confirm the total and the tip, ideally seeing the amount before it is finalized. Anything else, even by accident, damages trust in a way that is very hard to repair, especially with a regular who has been generous over time.

## Not Listening: Repainted Toes and an Argument Over a New Set

The quality and communication added to it. The client said she has had to have her toes repainted twice because the polish was applied messily, and she was clear she is not overly picky. Then, on this visit, she asked for a brand-new set with tips, explaining that she does a lot of yard work and gets dirt under her nails, so she wanted a fresh start. The technician instead insisted on simply applying acrylic over her existing nails and argued that this counted as a new set, only agreeing to do it with tips after the client said she could just go next door. When a client explains what she wants and why, the right response is to do exactly that, not to argue her out of it. Her reason was practical and valid, and honoring it should have been simple.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that each of these is fixable, and this is a customer worth keeping. First, protect tip integrity absolutely: the client always chooses and confirms the tip, and staff never enter one, even when the card reader is acting up. Second, keep the quality clean so a client is not sent back for repaints. Third, listen to the client and honor her request and her reasoning, doing the new set with tips she asked for without an argument. And treat a loyal regular, especially one spending around $300 a month, as the valued customer she is, never as a bother for wanting her nails to look good. With honest billing, clean work, and a willingness to listen, this long-loyal client would have stayed instead of walking next door.
