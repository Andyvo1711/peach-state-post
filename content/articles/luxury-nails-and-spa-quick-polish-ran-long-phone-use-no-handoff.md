A quick polish change is one of the simplest services a salon offers, and clients usually book it because they are short on time. A recent review of Luxury Nails & Spa comes from a customer who stopped in for exactly that, and left feeling that her time had not been respected. Her account is a useful reminder that even the shortest service depends on the technician's full attention.

## A Quick Polish That Took Much Longer

According to the review, the customer came in for a simple polish, a service she expected to be fast. Instead, she says it took well over half an hour. For a client who has planned her day around a brief appointment, that kind of delay can be frustrating, regardless of how the final result looks.

When a service runs longer than expected, a short update helps a great deal. A quick "Thank you for your patience, I will be finished shortly" tells the client that the team knows her time matters. It also gives her the chance to say whether she is able to wait, which is especially helpful for guests who came in on a lunch break or between other plans.

## Phone Use and Presence During Service

The customer shared that the technician kept her phone in her lap and was using it throughout the visit. She explained that the technician mentioned she had lost her luggage and needed to speak with someone about it. The customer was understanding about that, noting "that's fine," but felt the situation should have been handled differently.

Personal emergencies happen to everyone, and teams should be supportive of one another. At the same time, clients notice when attention is divided, and a phone in a technician's lap can feel like a signal that the guest is not the priority. Many salons find it helpful to set a simple standard, such as keeping phones away from the station during services and stepping aside for urgent calls. A technician can say, "I am sorry, I need to take an important call. May I have a colleague finish your service?" That brief sentence keeps the guest informed and respected.

## Handing Off When Something Urgent Comes Up

The most practical point in the review is the customer's own suggestion: "someone else should have done my nails if she was busy." It is a fair observation. When a technician needs time for an urgent matter, a handoff to another team member is often the best way to protect both the client's schedule and the technician's situation.

A manager or front desk lead can help by checking whether any colleague is free, offering to move the client to another chair, or letting the technician step away for a few minutes while someone else takes over. Making this a normal option, with no stigma for the person who asks, helps teams handle difficult days smoothly. It also gives the client a clear way forward, rather than a long and uncertain wait.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers three lessons for nail teams. First, treat every service, including a quick polish, as a commitment to the client's time, and give a short update whenever it is running long. Second, keep phones away from the station during services and step aside for urgent calls, with a brief explanation to the guest. Third, build a simple handoff process, so that a colleague can step in whenever a technician needs to attend to something important.

Clients are generally understanding when life gets in the way. What they appreciate most is a team that notices, communicates, and finds a way to take care of them anyway.
