A visit to Lavish Nails Salon Johns Creek left a nearby customer with mixed feelings, according to her account. She was happy with the manicure work itself and had even been considering making the salon her regular spot given how close it is to home. Instead, she says a billing mix-up over her pedicure service left her hesitant to return, a reminder of how much a single moment at checkout can outweigh an otherwise strong visit.

## A Good Manicure Experience, Genuinely Appreciated

The client was clear that the technician who did her nails did a good job, and that convenience and quality had her ready to become a repeat customer. It is worth salons recognizing and reinforcing this kind of skilled, attentive work, since it is often exactly what turns a first-time visitor into a loyal one. When a technician is doing strong work, the rest of the visit, especially the parts around pricing and payment, should ideally support that impression rather than undercut it.

## When a Requested Service Doesn't Match the Receipt

According to the client, she specifically asked for a sports pedicure, priced at $35, and says the technician suggested a deluxe pedicure instead, an upgrade she declined at the time. When she checked her receipt after paying, she found she had been charged for the deluxe service rather than the one she had requested and agreed to. This kind of gap between a spoken request and what ends up on the final bill is one of the more damaging issues a salon can run into, since it can leave a customer feeling that pricing isn't being handled transparently, even if the mismatch was an honest mix-up rather than anything intentional. A simple habit of confirming the exact service and price out loud before starting, and again at checkout, would help ensure the ticket always reflects what the customer actually chose. Having pedicure tiers clearly listed at each station or on a laminated card can also reduce the chance of upselling confusion during the appointment itself.

## Editorial Perspective

A few takeaways are worth carrying forward from this visit. First, when a client declines an upgrade, that decision should be reflected clearly on the final ticket, not just remembered verbally in the moment. Second, confirming the service and price with the customer before checkout, rather than after, gives her a chance to catch and correct any mismatch on the spot rather than after leaving. Third, this case is a good example of how strong technical work can still be overshadowed by a billing issue; the salon clearly has the skill to earn repeat business, and tightening up how services are confirmed and charged would help make sure that skill isn't undercut by an avoidable mistake.
