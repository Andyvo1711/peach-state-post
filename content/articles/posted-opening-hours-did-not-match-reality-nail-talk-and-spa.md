Nail Talk & Spa recently received a review from a longtime customer who said she typically loves the service there. That kind of loyalty is what every salon works to earn, and it makes her feedback especially valuable. Her concern was not about the manicure or the team's skill. It was about a simple question that every client asks before leaving home: what time do they open?

## When the Hours Do Not Match

According to the customer, both the website and the sign on the door said the salon opened at 9:00 a.m. She stood outside from 9:00 until about 9:15 and, with no sign of anyone opening up, decided to call. She was told the salon opened at 10:00 and that the team had not yet "changed the door." In her words, this was "not acceptable," and she was not sure she would return.

A change in hours is a normal business decision, and clients are usually understanding about it. The difficulty comes when the information they rely on is out of date. A client who plans a morning around a 9:00 appointment may have arranged childcare, taken time from work, or driven across town. Even a short wait outside can feel like a larger disrespect of her time when the posted hours said something different.

## A Quick Checklist for Updating Hours

Salons can protect clients with a short routine whenever hours change. The door sign, website, Google Business profile, social media pages, and any booking app should all be updated on the same day, ideally by one person who is responsible for keeping them aligned. A monthly check, where a team member reads each listing aloud and compares it with the real schedule, catches most mismatches before a client does. If the change is temporary, a short note on the door and a line on the website saying so is enough.

It also helps to tell regular clients directly. A text message or a note at checkout, such as "Starting next week, we open at 10:00 a.m.," takes a minute to send and shows that the salon values the people who return. For clients already waiting outside, a kind reply on the phone matters too. A sincere apology, a thank-you for calling, and perhaps an offer to book the next visit at a convenient time can turn a frustrating morning into a moment of good service.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a reminder that trust is built from small details, and opening hours are one of the first. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, update the door sign, website, and online listings together whenever hours change, and assign one person to own that task. Second, let regular clients know about changes directly, since they are the most likely to plan around the old schedule. Third, when a client calls about a mismatch, respond with a warm apology and a clear next step so that a loyal customer feels valued.

With accurate hours and a little extra communication, a salon can keep the goodwill of the clients who already love its work.
