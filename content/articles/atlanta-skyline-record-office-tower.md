A new 58-story office tower is rising in Midtown Atlanta, set to become the tallest addition to the city's skyline in more than a decade, developers announced this week, driven largely by continued corporate relocations from higher-cost metros.

The tower, developed by a consortium of regional real estate investors, has already pre-leased roughly 55 percent of its office space to financial services and technology tenants, several of which are consolidating operations previously split across multiple cities. Developers say the building was designed specifically with large open floor plates in mind, a format increasingly difficult to find in older Atlanta office stock.

"Atlanta stopped being a fallback option years ago and became a first choice for companies that need large, modern floor plates at a lower cost basis than the coasts," said one developer involved in the project who requested anonymity ahead of a formal leasing announcement. "This tower exists because we had tenants asking for exactly this kind of space before we ever broke ground."

The project is expected to be completed within three years and will add roughly 1.3 million square feet of office space to the Midtown submarket. City officials have pointed to the tower as continued evidence of Atlanta's transformation into one of the country's leading corporate relocation destinations.

Local economists caution that continued office tower construction depends heavily on corporate relocation trends remaining strong, noting that a broader pullback in office-based hiring could leave newly built towers with harder-to-fill vacancies.
