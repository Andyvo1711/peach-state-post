Julian Marsh, a Georgia Tech-trained mechanical engineer, opened a new robotics manufacturing facility in Augusta this month for his company Ironrail Automation South, which builds warehouse automation robots used for pallet handling and inventory movement.

Marsh spent nearly a decade working at established robotics firms before founding the company four years ago, betting that a wave of manufacturers looking to reduce dependence on overseas supply chains would create sustained demand for domestically built automation hardware. The Augusta facility, a renovated former industrial building, will employ 75 people at full capacity, combining precision manufacturing roles with software engineering positions.

"Every warehouse operator I talk to has the same anxiety about supply chain disruption, and automation is one of the few tools that actually reduces their dependence on any single overseas supplier," Marsh said. "Building the robots themselves domestically was a logical next step, not just a patriotic talking point."

The company chose Augusta in part due to available industrial real estate at a fraction of the cost of comparable space in Metro Atlanta, along with state manufacturing tax incentives Marsh says made the location financially competitive. The company has raised $20 million in venture funding to date.

Marsh says the company's current order backlog extends roughly seven months, driven largely by mid-sized logistics companies seeking to automate operations without the multi-year capital commitments required by larger automation vendors.
