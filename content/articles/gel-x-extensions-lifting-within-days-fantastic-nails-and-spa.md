A walk-in customer shared a thoughtful review of Fantastic Nails & Spa, and much of it was positive. They said they were greeted right away, that everyone was very friendly, and that the finished set looked pretty on the day of the visit. They chose the salon for its location, its prices, and its customer service, and said they would likely return for another service. Their concern was about how long the gel x set lasted, which makes this a helpful case for any team that offers extensions.

## A Walk-In Visit and a Shared Service

The customer arrived on a Sunday without an appointment and said only one technician available could do gel x, and that technician was busy with another customer. A friendly team member began the preparation so the visit could keep moving, and the customer later moved to the gel x technician to complete the set, which took about an hour. They noted that they had asked for a regular gel manicure, but were told gel x would be fine.

Splitting a service between two team members can keep walk-ins moving, but it works best when the handoff is clear. The technician who finishes the set can review the preparation, check that the nail surface is properly cleaned and dehydrated, and confirm that the customer is comfortable with the service chosen. When a customer asks for one service and is guided toward another, a short explanation of the difference in wear and care helps them make an informed decision.

## Nails That Did Not Last

The customer said that the day after the visit, a nail fell off while washing their hands, and another came off the following day. They reattached the first with nail glue they happened to have with them. They said they paid $80 and, a week later, had replaced three nails with glue and had two more lifting. In their experience, gel x from other salons has lasted about a month.

Early lifting often points to the preparation or adhesion stage. Careful removal of oils and shine from the natural nail, correct tip sizing and fit, and thorough curing all help extensions stay in place. A quick firmness check at the end of the service, gently pressing each nail edge, can catch weak spots before the customer leaves.

## Aftercare and Follow-Up

The customer was away on a trip when the nails began to lift, and said they felt the team might have fixed them had they been in town. Salons can bridge this gap by giving a short aftercare note, such as how to treat the nails in the first days and how to reach the salon if something lifts. Offering a repair window for new gel x sets shows the salon stands behind its work, even when the customer cannot return right away.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers several lessons. First, confirm the service with the customer before beginning, and explain the differences between a gel manicure and gel x, especially for walk-ins who are not sure what they need. Second, make handoffs clear, so the technician finishing a set checks the preparation and tests each nail for firmness. Third, give every customer simple aftercare guidance and a repair window for early lifting. A friendly welcome and a good-looking set are real strengths, and pairing them with lasting results helps turn a good first impression into a returning customer.
