A client's visit to Snazzy Nail Lounge highlights how important it is to confirm what a salon can actually deliver before starting a service, not just once but at each step where it matters. She says she took extra care to check in advance, and the outcome still did not match what she had been told to expect.

## Confirming a Design That Couldn't Be Delivered

The client says she showed the technician a reference photo before starting and asked directly whether the colors could be matched, and was told yes. She then specifically asked again to confirm the chrome color was available, and again was told yes. Partway through the service, however, she was told the gold needed for the design was not in stock. Checking supply availability against a client's reference photo before beginning, rather than partway through, would have given her the chance to choose a different design or color combination from the start. When a substitution becomes necessary mid-service, walking the client through the available alternatives in the moment, rather than proceeding without her input, helps avoid a result that does not match what she agreed to.

## Uneven Nail Shapes

The client also noticed that one hand had been shaped round while the other was shaped almond. To her credit, the technician corrected the rounded hand once the client pointed out the difference, which is the right response when a client catches something like this. Even so, having both hands checked for a matching shape before the client needs to flag it herself would prevent this kind of inconsistency from happening in the first place, since shape mismatches are relatively easy to catch with a quick side-by-side comparison partway through the service.

## Responding to a Dissatisfied Client

The client says that even after voicing her disappointment with the final result, she did not feel there was meaningful effort made to address it. When a client clearly communicates that the outcome does not match what was promised, offering a concrete next step, such as a partial redo, a price adjustment, or simply talking through what happened, tends to matter more to the client than any explanation of why the mismatch occurred. Leaving a visibly unhappy client without any resolution is likely to cost more in future business than the time it would take to make a reasonable offer in the moment.

## Editorial Perspective

A few takeaways stand out here. First, when a client shows a reference photo, checking every element of it, including specific colors, against actual current stock before the service begins is the clearest way to avoid promising something that cannot be delivered. Second, side-by-side shape checks partway through a service catch inconsistencies before the client has to point them out herself. Third, when a client expresses dissatisfaction with the final result, offering a clear resolution in the moment, rather than letting the conversation end without one, is often what determines whether she gives the salon another chance.
