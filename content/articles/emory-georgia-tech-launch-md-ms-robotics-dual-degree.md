Emory University School of Medicine and the Georgia Institute of Technology have launched a dual-degree program that pairs a Doctor of Medicine from Emory with a Master of Science in Robotics from Georgia Tech, Emory announced on September 22.

## How the Program Works

The five-year MD/MS in Robotics sends admitted students to Georgia Tech for their first year, where they take core courses, professional-preparation seminars and electives covering mechanics, controls, perception, artificial intelligence and human-robot interaction. They then spend four years at Emory in medical school while finishing remaining Georgia Tech requirements, including a capstone project.

## Who It Targets

Program leaders expect four to six students per year, drawn mainly from engineering, biomedical engineering, robotics or computer science backgrounds. Emory's Dr. Jason Bariteau, who is helping lead the pathway, said the aim is to prepare physicians who can both identify clinical problems and help build the technology to solve them. The school describes the program as one of the first of its kind in the country.
