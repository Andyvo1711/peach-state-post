State economists released a new economic forecast this week projecting steady, moderate growth for Georgia through 2027, citing continued strength in logistics, healthcare, and financial services as the primary drivers expected to sustain the expansion.

The forecast projects gross state product growth roughly in line with or slightly above the national average over the projection period, a continuation of a trend that has positioned Georgia as one of the faster-growing large state economies in the Southeast. Economists attributed the strong outlook partly to continued corporate relocation into Atlanta and partly to sustained strength in the state's port logistics and film production sectors.

"Georgia has quietly become one of the more resilient state economies in the country, not because of one dominant industry but because of how many sectors are pulling in the same direction," said a state fiscal economist who presented the forecast. "We're not projecting explosive growth, but steady, broad-based growth across multiple sectors is arguably a healthier outcome than a boom concentrated in a single industry."

The forecast flagged several risk factors that could alter the projection, including potential interest rate changes, national economic conditions, and the pace of continued corporate relocation into Atlanta. Officials emphasized that the projection assumes no major economic disruption over the forecast period.

Budget officials say the forecast will inform revenue projections used in upcoming state budget planning, though they cautioned that economic forecasts of this length carry meaningful uncertainty.
