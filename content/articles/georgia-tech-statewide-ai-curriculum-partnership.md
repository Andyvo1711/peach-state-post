Georgia Institute of Technology has launched a partnership with twelve Georgia-based employers to rebuild core sections of its computer science curriculum around applied artificial intelligence, university officials announced this week.

Under the arrangement, participating companies will send engineers to co-teach elective courses, provide real anonymized datasets for student projects, and guarantee interview slots for students who complete a new applied-AI certificate track. Georgia Tech says the goal is to close a persistent gap between classroom instruction and the skills employers say new graduates actually need on the job.

"We kept hearing the same complaint from hiring managers: graduates understood the theory but had never touched a messy, real-world dataset," said Georgia Tech's Interim Dean of Computing. "This partnership puts that experience inside the degree itself, not as an afterthought internship."

The certificate track will launch as a pilot with 180 students this fall before expanding to the full computer science and data science programs next year. Employers involved in the partnership span logistics, financial services, and healthcare technology — three industries that recruit heavily from Georgia Tech's Atlanta campus.

University officials say the redesigned curriculum was shaped in part by employer survey data showing that most entry-level technical hires require six to nine months of additional training before becoming fully productive. Georgia Tech is betting the new coursework can cut that ramp-up period roughly in half.
