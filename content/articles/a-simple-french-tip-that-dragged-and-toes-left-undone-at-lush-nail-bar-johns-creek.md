When two clients start the same service at the same time, a big gap in how long it takes stands out immediately. This report on Lush Nail Bar Johns Creek describes exactly that: two friends who got the same pedicure deal, and one who ended up waiting well beyond the other for a simple finish, then left without it done. The issues are about pacing, skill on the basics, and how the salon handled the result.

## A Simple Service That Fell Far Behind

The client came in with her best friend, and they chose the same pedicure special and started at the same time. Her friend finished and went to wait in the car, while the client sat for more than twenty additional minutes just to have a simple French tip completed. A basic French tip should not stretch a service that far past an identical one that began at the same moment. Keeping pace consistent, so two clients on the same service finish around the same time, is part of respecting everyone's schedule, and a simple design like a French tip is exactly the kind of work that should move smoothly.

## A Redo, and Leaving With Toes Undone

The delay was compounded by the result. By the client's account, the technician made a mistake on one foot, and the owner then removed all the polish from that foot to start over. Having already been there far longer than she expected, the client asked them to remove the polish from the other foot as well, and left with her toes not done at all, having paid $50 for the basic cucumber pedicure special. When a redo is needed, handling it quickly matters so the client is not stuck much longer, and if she ends up leaving without the service finished, that is the moment to make it right on the price. Charging full price for a special that sent her home with bare toes is what turned the frustration into a decision not to return.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that all of this is about pacing, fundamentals, and fairness. First, keep service times consistent, especially for two clients on the same deal, so no one is left waiting long after their companion for a simple finish. Second, build reliable skill on the basics like a French tip, so a foot does not need to be stripped and restarted. Third, when a mistake means a redo, handle it efficiently, and if the client still leaves without the service completed, adjust the price or offer a free return rather than charging full for an unfinished pedicure. With steady pacing and a fair response, these two friends could have finished together and both left happy, instead of one leaving with bare toes and a warning to others.
