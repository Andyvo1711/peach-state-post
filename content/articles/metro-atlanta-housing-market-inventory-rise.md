Metro Atlanta's housing inventory grew for the first time in three years this spring, according to data released by a coalition of regional real estate associations, offering modest relief to buyers who have faced a persistently tight market driven by limited new construction and homeowners reluctant to give up low mortgage rates locked in years earlier.

The inventory increase was most pronounced in outer suburban counties, where realtors say a combination of new construction completions and an uptick in retirees downsizing has finally begun easing supply constraints. Home price growth, while still positive, slowed to its lowest pace in several years alongside the inventory gains.

"Buyers have spent three years getting used to bidding wars and waived inspections, so even a modest increase in available homes feels significant to people who've been searching for a while," said a regional real estate association spokesperson who presented the data. "We're not calling this a buyer's market yet, but it's the first real crack in what has been an extremely tight seller's market."

Housing economists caution that mortgage rate movements remain the single largest variable shaping the market's trajectory, noting that any significant rate decline could quickly reignite the kind of competitive bidding conditions that have defined the market in recent years.

The inventory gains were less pronounced in Atlanta's most in-demand intown neighborhoods, including areas near the Beltline, where housing supply remains constrained by limited available land for new construction.
