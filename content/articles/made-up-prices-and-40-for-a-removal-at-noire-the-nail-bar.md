Two things quietly decide whether a client trusts a salon: whether the prices feel consistent and fair, and how the salon treats them when something goes wrong. This report on Noire The Nail Bar touches both. A customer facing an emergency was charged an amount she found hard to explain, and then met with attitude as she left, an experience that stuck with someone who has been getting her nails done for years.

## Prices That Felt Made Up on the Spot

The client's core frustration was pricing that seemed invented in the moment, or as she put it, "made up off the hop." After she'd been in the chair only about five minutes, the salon removed the gel from her toes and feet and tried to charge her $40 for that removal alone. As an experienced client, she said she'd never seen a take-off-only service priced that way, and it left her appalled. Removal is a legitimate service that can carry a fee, but that fee needs to be posted and consistent, not produced on the spot. When a price appears out of nowhere, even a fair number feels arbitrary.

## An Emergency Met With Attitude

What made it worse was the human side. The client had an emergency and had to leave, and when she explained that she needed to run out, she got attitude from everyone. That's the most avoidable part of the whole visit. Emergencies happen, and a client who has to leave through no fault of the salon deserves understanding, not coldness. How a salon treats someone in a difficult moment is exactly what they remember and repeat.

## Editorial Perspective

The fixes here are straightforward and build trust rather than cost it. First, post clear, consistent pricing, including for removals and partial services, so no number is ever a surprise, and quote it before any work begins. Second, price fairly for what was actually done: a removal cut short by an emergency after a few minutes calls for a reasonable, transparent charge, not a figure that feels pulled from the air. Third, and most important, meet difficult moments with grace. A client leaving for a genuine emergency should be sent off with understanding and a warm "we hope everything's okay, come back and see us." Handled that way, an interrupted visit becomes a reason to return, instead of a review warning others away.
