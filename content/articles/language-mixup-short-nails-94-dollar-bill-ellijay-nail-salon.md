A client visited Ellijay Nail Salon for a straightforward nail service and left with two main concerns, one tied to how her preferences were understood during the appointment, and one tied to the final price. She does note that the technician did a nice job with the polish itself, but the rest of the visit left her unlikely to return.

## A Communication Gap That Affected the Result

The client says the technician had difficulty understanding what she was asking for, and as a result her real nails ended up clipped shorter than she wanted. She mentions trying to be understanding about the mix-up in the moment. When a client and technician don't fully understand each other, pausing to confirm length and shape before any clipping or filing begins, using gestures, a mirror, or even a quick sketch if needed, can prevent an outcome the client didn't ask for. Having a staff member nearby who can help bridge a language gap during these moments also gives clients more confidence that their preferences will be followed.

## An Add-On That Wasn't Fully Explained

The client says she was offered eyebrow and lip waxing as an add on for what she understood to be $15 extra, and agreed since it seemed like a small addition. She felt the waxing itself was done carelessly. Whenever an add on service is offered, walking the client through what it involves and roughly how long it will take helps set the right expectations, and taking the same care on a smaller add on service as on the main appointment matters just as much to how the client feels about the visit overall.

## A Final Price That Didn't Match Expectations

The client describes her main service as a plain polish job on both her fingers and toes, with no tips, dipping, or other enhancements. She was surprised to be charged $94 total once the waxing was included, and felt the price didn't reflect what she'd received. Being upfront about the full cost before starting, especially once an add on has been agreed to, gives the client a chance to confirm the total or ask questions before the service is finished rather than discovering it at checkout. A simple verbal rundown of the price for each service included, given either before starting or right before payment, helps avoid this kind of surprise.

## Editorial Perspective

A few lessons come out of this visit. First, when communication is difficult, slowing down and confirming details like length and shape before any cutting begins protects the client's preferences and the salon's reputation alike. Second, add on services deserve the same care and explanation as the main appointment, since a rushed extra can color a client's impression of the whole visit. Third, walking a client through the total price before the service wraps up, particularly when add ons are involved, helps ensure the final bill doesn't come as an unwelcome surprise.
