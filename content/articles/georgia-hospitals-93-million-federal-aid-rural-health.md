The federal government is delivering $93.3 million to Georgia through the Rural Health Transformation Program, aimed at helping rural hospitals adopt new care models, according to a CMS announcement reported by Atlanta News First and WTOC.

## What It Covers

The investment will help 87 rural hospitals prepare for value-based care, expand training and recruitment for nurses, EMTs and paramedics, and improve access to maternal and behavioral health services and newborn screening through telehealth, transportation and expanded care networks. It will also bring surgical robotics, dementia care capacity and telehealth infrastructure to rural communities.

## A History of Closures

Faris Flournoy, CEO of Flournoy Health Systems, grew up in Hancock County, where Hancock Memorial Hospital closed in 2001 and more than 9,000 people lost their closest hospital, according to a state audit report. He said new federal money alone will not fix what rural Georgia lost. The Georgia Hospital Association has found 18 hospitals closed statewide since 2013, and a 2025 Chartis Center for Rural Health analysis counted 22 Georgia hospitals at risk of closing.
