In Lavonia, a rural community in northeast Georgia, the hospital no longer delivers babies. Kara Holden, a former obstetric nurse, made the hourlong drive to Greenville, South Carolina, on October 11 to give birth to her son, KFF Health News reported.

## A Shrinking Map of Care

St. Mary's Sacred Heart Hospital in Lavonia stopped providing obstetric services nearly a year ago, partly because of Medicaid cuts. In Georgia, only 36 percent of rural hospitals have a labor and delivery unit, and four have closed since 2020, according to the Center for Healthcare Quality and Payment Reform.

## Obstetric Carts

The state plans to use more than $12 million of the nearly $219 million approved for Georgia in the first year of the federal Rural Health Transformation Program to place obstetric carts in 43 rural hospitals without labor and delivery units. The carts hold supplies for a birth or obstetric emergency, including neonatal resuscitation tools, thermal blankets and medications such as oxytocin and magnesium sulfate.

## Concerns

South Georgia OB-GYN Justin Peterson said the carts sound fine in theory but would put supplies in the hands of emergency doctors without extensive obstetric experience. He wants the state to invest more in preventing labor and delivery closures.
