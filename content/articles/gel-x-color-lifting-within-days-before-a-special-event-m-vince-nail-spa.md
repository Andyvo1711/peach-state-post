M. Vince' Nail Spa recently received a review from a customer who booked a Gel X set with gel color on top and a manicure. She chose the service for a reason many clients share: she had an event coming up and wanted her nails to look their best. Her account is a useful reminder that for many guests, a manicure is tied to a date on the calendar, and that makes durability especially important.

## When the Color Does Not Last

According to the customer, she visited on a Wednesday, and by Saturday the color had come off her nails entirely. She said she invested a good amount in the set and manicure, and that she was disappointed to see it change within three days. With an event the same day, she did not have time to repair her nails beforehand.

Gel color that lifts early often points to the preparation or curing steps. Moisture or oils on the nail plate, product placed too close to the skin, thick layers, or a cure that was too short can all reduce adhesion. A helpful routine includes thorough cleansing, gentle surface prep, a dehydrator or primer where suitable, thin and even coats, and full curing for each layer, with the free edge sealed at the end. Many technicians also check the lamp's output regularly, since older bulbs can cure less effectively.

## Planning Around Special Occasions

Clients often schedule their appointments close to events, which leaves little room for a repair. A short question at booking, such as, "Do you have an event coming up?" lets the team plan accordingly. For occasion nails, technicians can suggest booking a few days early, add a second top coat, and discuss which finishes tend to last. A quick look at the nails at the end of the visit, with the client's hands in natural light, also helps confirm that the finish is smooth and sealed.

## Aftercare and a Repair Window

Clear aftercare guidance can extend the life of a set. Technicians can remind clients to avoid hot water and heavy cleaning for the first several hours, wear gloves for chores, and use cuticle oil daily. Posting a simple repair policy, such as a free fix within a set number of days, gives clients a clear next step. For a client with an upcoming event, offering a same-day or priority repair slot can turn a stressful morning into a moment that builds loyalty.

## Editorial Perspective

This case is a reminder that a manicure often carries a deadline, and clients are trusting the salon to deliver on it. Three practical lessons stand out.

First, prepare each nail carefully and cure every layer fully, so the color stays smooth and secure from the first day. Second, ask clients about upcoming events when they book, and plan the timing and finish to match. Third, share aftercare tips and a clear repair option, including priority slots for clients who need a quick fix.

When a salon plans for the moments clients care about most, it earns the trust that brings them back.
