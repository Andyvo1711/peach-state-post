A recent gel-X visit to V Nails & Spa left a longtime client disappointed enough to write a review, even though she said she does not usually like leaving negative feedback. Her set lifted in less than a week, well short of the four weeks she typically gets from the same type of service elsewhere, and she pointed to a specific technical step she noticed was missing along the way. Her account is a useful reminder of how much a few small prep steps affect the final result.

## A Set That Did Not Hold Up

The client's core complaint was durability. Gel-X sets are designed to hold for several weeks when applied correctly, so a set popping off within days almost always points back to the application process rather than bad luck. She also noted that the technician worked very quickly, which is worth paying attention to, because speed and thoroughness can work against each other during enhancement services. A helpful practice is to treat gel-X application as a fixed sequence of steps with a realistic minimum time attached, rather than a task to finish as fast as possible, especially during busy periods when the temptation to rush is highest.

## Skipping Primer and Dehydrator

The client specifically noticed that no primer or dehydrator was used before the gel adhesive went on, a step she said other salons include as standard. This step matters because it removes oils and moisture from the natural nail and helps the gel bond properly, and skipping it is one of the more common reasons enhancements lift early. Building a short, visible checklist at each station for enhancement services, prep, dehydrator, primer, and application in that order, gives technicians a consistent standard to follow regardless of how full the schedule is. Having a senior technician periodically spot-check that these steps are being followed can catch shortcuts before they turn into repeat complaints.

## Finish Quality and What the Client Pays For

Beyond durability, the client said the end result looked sloppy and that she had to file the edges herself once she got home. A finished set should not need touch-ups at the client's kitchen table. Before a client leaves the chair, a quick final pass, checking edges under good lighting, running a finger along the sides, and asking the client directly if everything feels smooth, catches these issues while they are still easy to fix. This step also shapes how a client judges value for money. The client in this case felt the service was priced above what she received, and a visible final quality check is one of the simplest ways to close that gap between price and perceived quality.

## Editorial Perspective

This case points to three practical lessons. First, gel-X durability depends heavily on following every prep step in order, and primer or dehydrator should never be skipped to save time, no matter how busy the salon gets. Second, working quickly is only a benefit if the quality holds up, so building a consistent, unhurried process for enhancement services protects both the client's nails and the salon's reputation. Third, a short final check before the client leaves, both for durability and for smooth, finished edges, is a small investment that prevents at-home touch-ups and helps clients feel the price they paid matched the result they received.
