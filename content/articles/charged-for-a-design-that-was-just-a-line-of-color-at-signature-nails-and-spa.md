A clean salon and a fair, skilled service should go together, and this report on Signature Nails & Spa is about a visit where the space was praised but the design and the way a concern was handled fell short. The client was clear that the shop itself was clean, which is a real credit, and her disappointment was about skill, pricing, and customer care. All three are within a salon's control.

## A Design Request That Became a Line of Color

The client asked for a design on her big toe. Instead, by her account, she received only a thin line of color at the tip of each toe, rather than the toes being fully painted or any real design being done. She felt the technician simply did not know how to do the design and would have been better off saying so upfront. There also seemed to be a communication gap, possibly a language difference, and she said she asked several times whether that was all that would be done before being told the service was finished. There is no shame in a technician not offering a particular design, but the honest, trust-building move is to say clearly at the start, "I'm not able to do that design, here is what I can offer," so the client can decide before any work begins.

## Charged Extra, Then Not Made Right

The pricing turned disappointment into frustration. The client was charged $8 extra for the so-called design, and when she said she did not think the charge was fair and would not return if they insisted on it, the request to remove it was refused. She also felt the salon's later response to her review did not match what actually happened. Beyond the single charge, her larger point was that the business chose to do nothing to address an unhappy customer, which is what signals whether a salon truly focuses on service. When a design is not really delivered, the fair step is simply not to charge for it, and when a customer raises a concern, listening and making it right, rather than digging in, is what protects the relationship.

## Editorial Perspective

The encouraging part is that all of this is fixable, and the clean space is a strong starting point. First, be honest about skills: if a requested design cannot be done well, say so before starting and offer an alternative, so the client is never surprised by the result. Second, charge only for what is actually delivered, and never add a design fee for a line of color that was not the design requested. Third, take care of an unhappy customer: listen calmly, remove an unfair charge, and treat the moment as a chance to keep her, since that response is the real measure of customer service. And when replying to a review, keep it accurate and gracious. Handled with honesty, fair pricing, and genuine care, this visit in an already-clean salon could have ended with a satisfied client rather than a lost one.
