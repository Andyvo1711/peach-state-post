A mother-daughter pedicure is often a special outing, the kind of appointment that people remember for the time spent together. A recent review of Milano Nail Lounge comes from a customer who took her daughter for exactly that kind of treat. She began on a positive note, saying "the nails are pretty," but explained that the pricing conversation left her feeling the visit had not been handled fairly. Her account offers useful lessons about promotions and add-ons.

## Pretty Results and a Pricing Surprise

The customer's praise for the finished nails is worth noting. The technical work and the look of the result were not her concern, and that is a strong foundation for any salon. What shaped her overall impression was the way pricing came together at the end.

According to her review, she was encouraged to choose the luxury pedicure for herself and her daughter after a team member said it was on sale for half off. At checkout, she says, she was told the service was actually full price. The total came to about $300, which she said was not what she had planned to spend. She was careful to add that the amount mattered less than the feeling that she and her daughter had been taken advantage of, a reminder that clients value honesty about price as much as the price itself.

## Promotions That Are Clear at the Point of Sale

When a promotion is mentioned during a service recommendation, clients treat it as part of the agreement. Any confusion about whether a discount applies can quickly turn a friendly upsell into a trust issue, even if the misunderstanding is innocent.

Salons can avoid this with a few easy habits. Promotions can be posted on a sign or menu with clear dates, eligible services, and any conditions. Team members can confirm the final price out loud before the service begins, for example, "The luxury pedicure is this price today, so the total for two will be this amount. Does that work for you?" If a mistake is discovered at checkout, a manager can step in, explain what happened, and offer to honor the quoted price or provide a fair adjustment. Making things right in the moment is almost always less costly than losing a family's future visits.

## Confirming Add-Ons, Especially for Younger Guests

The customer also shared that a cat eye effect was applied to her daughter's toes without checking with her first. Add-ons and special finishes are fun, and many young clients love them, but they often come with an additional charge, and a parent usually expects to approve any extra cost.

A short confirmation takes only a few seconds. A technician can show the option, mention the price, and ask the parent or guardian to agree before starting. This also helps the younger guest feel included, since she can choose the look with her parent's approval. For any guest, a simple rule is to confirm every extra before it is applied.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers three lessons for nail teams. First, make promotions clear and visible, and state the exact price out loud before any recommended service begins. Second, confirm every add-on and special finish with the paying client in advance, especially when the guest is a child or teenager. Third, empower a manager to resolve pricing misunderstandings at checkout with a calm explanation and a fair solution.

Nails that look beautiful can still leave a guest hesitant to return if the price conversation felt unclear. Transparency is what lets the work speak for itself.
