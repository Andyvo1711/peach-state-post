A visit to the nail salon with a family member is usually a relaxed outing. A recent review of Fashion Nails comes from a daughter who accompanied her mother and left feeling the experience had been difficult for both of them. Her account describes a misunderstanding about a removal service, concerns about the condition of her mother's natural nails afterward, and a sense that the team's tone was not welcoming. Each of these points offers a lesson for salon teams.

## A Service Request Heard Two Different Ways

According to the review, the mother wanted to have the acrylic she was wearing soaked off. Instead, the reviewer says, the acrylic was popped off the nail bed. When the mother mentioned her concern and said she would simply pay, a team member responded that "if she wanted a soak off then she should've said soak off." The reviewer felt this placed the blame on her mother, who is a nail technician herself and, in her daughter's words, knows what to ask for.

Misunderstandings of this kind are common, because clients and technicians do not always use the same terms. A short confirmation before starting prevents them: "Would you like a full soak-off with acetone, or a different type of removal?" Using simple, clear language, and repeating the plan back to the client, helps both sides feel aligned. If something goes differently than expected, a calm "I am sorry, let us see how we can help" is far more effective than explaining how the request should have been worded.

## Removing Acrylic Gently

The reviewer reports that her mother left with nail beds that felt weak and flimsy, and that pieces of the natural nail surface had come away. Removing acrylic is one of the more delicate services in a salon, because the product bonds firmly to the natural nail, and forcing it can thin or damage the layers beneath.

The safest approach is to reduce the bulk of the acrylic with a file, soak the nails in acetone for the recommended time, and gently ease off the softened product, taking care not to pry or pull. Technicians can check each nail as they go, letting the soak continue when any resistance is felt. Finishing with a nourishing treatment, such as cuticle oil and a gentle buff, and sharing aftercare tips, such as keeping nails short and moisturized, helps natural nails recover. If any damage is noticed, telling the client right away and offering guidance builds trust.

## Tone, Laughter, and Respect at the Table

Finally, the reviewer shared that remarks were made in another language, followed by laughter among several team members, at two points during the visit, once at the start and once as her mother paid. She and her mother felt the comments were directed at them, and she described the overall tone as unwelcoming.

It is impossible to know what was intended, and the team may have been sharing an unrelated joke. Even so, guests are sensitive to this kind of moment, especially when they cannot follow the conversation. A helpful practice is to keep conversations between team members about clients, or any laughter that could be misread, away from the service area, and to speak to guests in a language they share. It also helps to remember that a client who works in the industry will often notice details closely and appreciates being treated as a respected peer.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers three lessons for nail teams. First, confirm the exact service in plain words before starting, and take responsibility for any misunderstanding instead of pointing to the wording. Second, remove acrylic gently with a proper soak, never prying it off, and offer aftercare so natural nails can recover. Third, keep conversations and laughter in the service area open and kind, so that every guest, including fellow professionals, feels welcome.

Clients rarely leave over a single moment. They leave when several small moments suggest that they are not valued.
