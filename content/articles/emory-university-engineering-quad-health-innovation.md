Emory University celebrated the opening of its new Health Innovation Engineering Quad this week, a $210 million complex that consolidates biomedical engineering and public health research programs previously scattered across several buildings on campus.

The four-building complex includes dedicated labs for wearable medical device prototyping, a health data science center, and simulation space for testing new surgical tools before they reach clinical trials. University officials say the quad was designed specifically to pair engineering faculty with public health and clinical researchers on shared projects rather than siloed departmental work.

"We wanted a building that forces collaboration instead of just permitting it," said Emory's Dean of Engineering at Tuesday's ribbon-cutting. "The best breakthroughs in health technology happen at the seams between disciplines, and this building is built around those seams."

The project was funded through a mix of university endowment spending, a $55 million gift from an anonymous alumni family, and a state grant tied to Georgia's health technology workforce goals. State officials attended the opening and pointed to the facility as part of a broader push to keep biomedical engineering graduates working in Georgia rather than relocating after graduation.

Roughly 45 faculty researchers and 320 graduate and undergraduate students will use the space starting this spring semester. The university says three new undergraduate courses tied to the facility's medical device labs will open for enrollment in the fall.
