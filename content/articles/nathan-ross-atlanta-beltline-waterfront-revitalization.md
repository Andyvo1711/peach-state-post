Nathan Ross, founder of development firm Ironline Partners, broke ground this week on a mixed-use project near the Atlanta Beltline aimed at revitalizing a long-underused stretch of land that has sat mostly vacant since a previous development effort collapsed more than a decade ago.

The project will include ground-floor retail, a public trail extension connecting to the Beltline, and residential units above street level, replacing a series of abandoned lots that have drawn complaints from neighboring business owners for years. Ross says the project was structured in phases specifically to avoid the funding gaps that doomed the site's previous redevelopment attempt.

"The last group that tried to build here ran out of money halfway through and left the neighborhood with a half-finished eyesore for ten years," Ross said. "We phased this project so that each stage is financially self-sustaining before we break ground on the next one. It's slower, but it's the only way I'd stake my company's name on a site with this history."

Ironline Partners secured financing through a combination of private equity investment and a city redevelopment grant tied to public trail access requirements. The first phase, expected to be complete within 18 months, will include the retail and trail components, with residential units following in a second phase.

Local business owners have cautiously welcomed the project, though several told city officials they are waiting to see visible progress before fully believing this redevelopment attempt will succeed where the last one failed.
